What's going on?

Perlin noise is a way to give randomness a mind — not jumpy and chaotic, but smooth and believable. Imagine asking many gentle neighbors for their opinion and averaging them — the result wiggles, but it’s continuous. That wiggle forms the main line you see moving across the canvas.

The sample dot floats along the curve to show one “reading” of the noise at a time, while the faint horizontal line divides high and low values, helping you see which parts are above or below the midpoint — like +ve and -ve regions.

In one sentence: Perlin noise gives you smooth randomness, shown as a rolling wave with a moving sample dot.

Mathematical Rose Pattern

“Don't just memorize — try to see it.” Imagine the curve as a family of paper petals arranged by a quiet conversation between two numbers, n and d. Change one; the petals whisper and rearrange. The math is simple: the radius wiggles like a cosine and the angle turns steadily — but the pattern that emerges is delightfully surprising. Tinker, observe, and ask why.

This sketch draws a rosette: points traced by r = R · cos(k·θ). By changing the integers n and d we change the fraction k = n/d and the shape flips between simple petals and intricate designs. Play with the sliders to get a feel for how a tiny change in a ratio produces strikingly different geometry.
